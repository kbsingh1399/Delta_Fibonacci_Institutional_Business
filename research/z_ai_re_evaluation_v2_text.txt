2. The 242-Asset Basket vs the Venue
The letter counts an asset "eligible" when one contract risks <= Rs 55 at the stop and the 10X margin is
<= Rs 2,500. That filter verifies against the live catalogue (250 perpetuals; 242-246 pass depending on
stop distance - the letter's 242 reproduces at a 2.5% stop). But listing eligibility is not tradability. At the
live pull of 14 Nov 2026 the binding constraints are liquidity and fee tier, and both cut the other way:
• Only 33 of 250 perpetuals turn over >= $1M per day; 82-85 turn over >= $100k. The remaining
~165 names are micro-cap listings where a Rs 2,500-8,000 notional order is a meaningful fraction of
daily flow.
• 200 of 250 perpetuals sit in the 0.05% taker tier (the letter's fee assumption for majors), versus 47 at
0.02% and 3 at 0.01%. Round-trip fees with GST are ~0.118% of notional - 0.118R at a 1% stop
before spread and slippage, versus the 0.03R the v2.0 Monte Carlo assumes.
• Five of the seven audited headline assets fail the $100k floor: ARMB USD 9,010/day, BANK USD
36,456, MRVL USD 48,817, GRAM USD 49,591, HOOD USD 92,581. Only XAUT ($167M) and
PAXG ($32M) pass cleanly. The basket "expansion" therefore adds illiquid names while the audited
core was already the liquid cream.
Exhibit 1: Deployable-universe funnel at the 14 Nov 2026 live pull (universe_screener.py, filters: 1-contract risk <= Rs 55, margin <=
Rs 2,500, 24h turnover >= $100k, fee cost <= 0.20R).
A second problem is silent in the letter: the master audit grid already searched all 249 symbols (11,613
trials). The 242-asset basket is not new discovery - it is a subset of an already-mined leaderboard.
Selecting the best per-symbol configurations from that grid and trading them wide is precisely the
multiple-testing failure mode the original audit flagged (F-05), now at 40x the selection breadth.

4. Monte Carlo Forensics: Where the Comfort Numbers
Come From
The v2.0 engine (engine/monte_carlo_portfolio_stress.py, commit 8a5b6d8) was executed and
reproduces audit/monte_carlo_stress_summary.csv exactly - the numbers are genuine outputs. The
problem is the model. Four defects, in descending order of severity:
• In-sample parameters hardcoded as truth. WR 46.5% and avg win +2.35R are the grid's average
of best-case selections, not forward expectations. The audited haircut (F-05) and the whole-lake
walk-forward (Section 3) both say expectancy decays ~6x out-of-sample.
• Friction 4x understated where it matters. The engine subtracts a flat 0.03R per trade. Measured
round-trip fees with GST are 0.116-0.141R at the audited stop distances, and 0.118R at a 1% stop for
the 0.05% taker tier that covers 200 of the 250 basket names. Spread and slippage are excluded
entirely.
• Drawdown horizon is one month, not the planning horizon. The 95th-percentile "max DD Rs
566.50" is the worst dip within a single 60-trade month. The dossier's own drawdown budget is a
six-month path; the original audit's engine measured six-month drawdown at 8.3-19.9R median across
its four scenarios (Rs 415-995 at Rs 50 = 1R).
• The "Cluster Shock" scenario is profitable by construction. It pays +7.05R at 42% versus -3R at
58% on 20 bundled trades: expectancy +1.22R per bundled unit, +24R per month - a "stress" that
makes money. A real 3R bunching event (three concurrent stops at -1.2R with slippage) is a -3.6R hit,
which is exactly what the removed crash-replay gate used to catch.
The 0.0% ruin probability follows from the same inputs: with +0.528R expected per trade, ruin is
impossible by assumption. With the measured friction and a halved edge (the audit's S3), the same
20,000-path machinery yields a median month near +Rs 1,380 and drawdowns of 15.9R median - three
times the v2.0 number. The defensible planning range remains the original audit's S3/S4 rows.
5. Governance: Two Regressions in the v2.0 Rewrite
The v2.0 modules are leaner than the delivered originals and wire cleanly into delta_live_scanner.py - but
two safety properties were lost in the rewrite, and one live-scanner change quietly diverges from every
backtest in the repository:
• Crash-replay gate removed. The v2.0 governor checks trade count, cluster membership, heat and
margin - but no longer replays the Nov 5 / Oct 14 tapes before approving. A three-position book of
correlated longs passes all four remaining checks and loses 3-3.6R in a single 4H candle, exactly the
event the gate existed to catch (and which the venue has now printed twice inside four weeks).

• Sizer floor inverted. The v2.0 sizer computes lots = max(1, round(target/current)) and labels
anything up to 2.5x the risk cap APPROVED_FLEX. A minimum-lot order that risks 2.5% of the
account is approved by default; the min-lot notional filter is gone. BTC at 1 contract with a 2.5% stop
risks Rs 135 (2.7%) - auto-approved. SOL is blocked only by the margin cap, not by design.
• Live scanner regime gate changed. delta_live_scanner.py now defines the macro regime as
EMA55 vs EMA89, while every backtest in the repository (single-TF and MTF, all 11,613 trials)
gates on EMA89 vs EMA233. The live signal generator therefore implements a strategy that has never
been tested. This must be reverted or the whole grid re-run; there is no third option.
What the rewrite got right is also worth recording: single-entry enforcement moved to a shared
active-positions file, the cluster map widened to five groups, and the 3R / 3-position / 60% margin stack
is a coherent posture for a Rs 5,000 book. The regressions are surgical fixes, not rewrites.

7. Updated Conditions for Live Deployment
The re-entry ladder from QR-2026-1114 stands, with the v2.0 findings folded in. In order:
• Fix the three regressions first (crash-replay gate, sizer APPROVED_FLEX path, scanner regime
gate). None is large; all three are currently live-code defects.
• Cap the basket at the 85 deployable names (screener output) and treat the $100k/day floor as a
standing filter, not a one-time study. Re-run the screener weekly.
• Paper trade the promotion registry. Zero symbols currently PASS. Accumulate forward trades
through the validator's registry; a symbol needs a rolling validate t >= 4.33 on forward data before it
earns live size.
• Re-state the plan economics from the S3/S4 rows (median ~Rs 1,200-1,400/month at 1% risk,
drawdown budget 15-20R) until forward evidence justifies anything better. The +32%/month and 0%
ruin figures should not appear in stakeholder material.
• Route every order through the new executor (dry-run first), with the governor wired as its
pre-trade hook and every decision logged. When a live fill happens, the trade log becomes the
out-of-sample dataset the program has never had.
The original audit's closing paragraph ages well: the constraint on this business is not signal generation,
capital efficiency or execution plumbing - all three now exist in production shape. The constraint is
proof. The path from here runs through accumulated forward evidence, and every tool delivered in this
set exists to make that evidence cheap to collect and hard to fake.
